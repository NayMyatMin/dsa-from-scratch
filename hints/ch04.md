# Hints — Chapter 4

**One tier at a time.** Every tier lives in its own file, so opening one cannot show you the next
— no matter how tall your terminal is or whether your editor renders Markdown. This page holds
links only; there is no hint text on it.

Read a tier, close it, go back to the code. If you read three tiers in a row you have read the
solution, and the problem is spent — you can't unsee it.

Before opening any tier, try this instead: both of these problems answer with a bare `True` or
`False`, which is the least informative failure there is — a wrong bool says nothing about which
step produced it. Print what your code actually found before you print what you concluded from it,
and do it on the shortest failing case in the test output rather than the longest. Section 7 of the
notes is why that pays here: more than one tool in this chapter reports "not there" with a value
that reads perfectly well as a hit, so a search that failed can look from the outside exactly like
one that succeeded. Roughly half of "I'm stuck" here turns out to be an answer read the wrong way,
or a bug you can see once the intermediate state is in front of you, not a missing idea.

The escalation is **nudge → observation → approach → full strategy**. Tier 4 hands over the
algorithm; treat opening it as spending the problem.

and 3 used: the answer is the returned `True` or `False` and nothing else, and `arr` has to come
back exactly as it was handed over. It is checked — each suite snapshots `arr` before the call and

docstring of each says so before any tier here does. Read both of those first: a tier written on
one problem's limits is the wrong tier for the other's.
