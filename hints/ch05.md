# Hints — Chapter 5

**One tier at a time.** Every tier lives in its own file, so opening one cannot show you the next
— no matter how tall your terminal is or whether your editor renders Markdown. This page holds
links only; there is no hint text on it.

Read a tier, close it, go back to the code. If you read three tiers in a row you have read the
solution, and the problem is spent — you can't unsee it.

Before opening any tier, try this instead: these three problems disagree with each other about
where the answer lives, and a solution that is right about the values and wrong about the address
fails with nothing on screen to say which half broke. After every attempt print three things — what
your method returned, what the list holds afterwards, and whether those two are the same object
under `is` — and check them against the stub's own contract paragraph rather than against the
examples. Section 7 of the notes is why: it takes two of the three contracts apart — 1299's and
283's, which are exact opposites — and shows that the disagreement is deliberate rather than
careless. Roughly half of "I'm stuck" here turns out to be a correct rewrite
delivered to the wrong place, or a bug you can see the moment the intermediate state is in front of
you, not a missing idea.

The escalation is **nudge → observation → approach → full strategy**. Tier 4 hands over the
algorithm; treat opening it as spending the problem.

Three stubs, three different answers to "what does the caller read". 1299 rewrites `arr` **and**
hands `arr` back, and its suite compares identity rather than contents, so a flawless fresh list
fails. 283 rewrites `nums` and must return `None`, so a flawless rewrite followed by `return nums`
fails. 905 is graded on the value you return and does not care what `nums` looks like afterwards, so
returning `None` fails there however perfect the list is. Each stub's docstring says which it wants,
in a block headed in capitals, before any tier here does — read that block first, because a tier
written for one problem's contract is the wrong tier for another's.

283 and 905 also disagree about order, and that disagreement is the reason both are in the chapter.
283 has exactly one accepted answer per input; 905 usually has many. Decide which of those you are
answering before you open anything.

The chapter's list in `PROGRESS.md` carries two more entries, 27 and 26, revisited here under the
in-place framing. They have no stubs of their own — they are the files in `arrays101/ch03/`, and
their tiers are on the [chapter 3 hints index](ch03.md).
